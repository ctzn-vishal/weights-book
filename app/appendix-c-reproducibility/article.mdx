A reader who wants to check the NHIS smoking standard error in Chapter 8 can obtain the same public-use file from IPUMS, read the same `build.py`, and still not run it, because the script expects a derived `current_smoker` column that lives in the author's store. "Every number comes from a script" and "a reader can rerun the script" are different promises. Four need keeping apart.

- **Public inputs are available.** Every headline result uses public-use files, from the producers or through IPUMS extracts, which require a free account and agreement to IPUMS terms.
- **Analysis code is available.** Each chapter's `build.py`, and its `verify.R` where one exists, is published in the `analysis/` folder of the site's repository.
- **The full preparation pipeline is not yet available.** The scripts that define the IPUMS extracts, download them, and clean them into the author's store, including derived outcome variables such as `uninsured` and `current_smoker`, live with the store, not in the repository.
- **Independent execution from a fresh setup is not yet possible.** The build scripts read the store's file paths and derived variables. A reader would need the same extracts, variable definitions, joins, and intermediate checks, and the booklet does not yet document all of them. Until it does, the tiers below describe the data, not a rebuild on a fresh machine.

## Data access tiers

Each result's inputs fall in one of three tiers. The tiers describe access to the data, not whether the analysis runs from the repository on a fresh machine.

- **Tier 1, public inputs.** The inputs are public-use files that anyone can obtain, either from the producers (Census Bureau, Bureau of Labor Statistics, National Center for Health Statistics, CDC) or through IPUMS extracts, which require a free account and agreement to IPUMS terms. Every headline result in the first edition is Tier 1.
- **Tier 2, inputs after registration.** Data whose producer requires an application or a data-use agreement beyond a free account. The PSID provenance box of Chapter 1 would be the only use in this edition, and the booklet does not reproduce its numbers.
- **Tier 3, released aggregates only.** None in the first edition.

The author's store and build scripts make the booklet reproducible for the author. For a reader, the four promises at the top of this appendix say what is and is not yet possible.

## Validation layers

A number reaches the page only after passing the layers that apply to it.

1. **Two implementations.** Design-based estimates and standard errors are computed through `svy`'s declared design and again with an independent hand-coded formula, and each chapter's manifest records the agreement.
2. **R `survey` sentinel checks.** Two or three headline design-based results per chapter are recomputed in R's `survey` package, the field's reference implementation, and the comparison is recorded in the chapter's manifest. Chapter 8's `verify.R` also sources the field guide's R declarations for NHIS, NHANES, BRFSS, ATUS, and CPS ASEC against the store, and confirms two documented failure modes: R stops on NHIS 2024 without `nest = TRUE`, and on the full BRFSS 2024 file without a lonely-PSU rule. The ACS declarations were checked only on a synthetic frame with the same column names, and the Stata declarations were not executed ([Chapter 8](/ch08-field-guide#how-to-read-a-guide)).
3. **Published benchmarks.** Each chapter compares its headline numbers with published figures and records the comparison in its manifest: ATUS work time with BLS, turnout with the Census Bureau, food insecurity with USDA, poverty rates with the Census Bureau, BRFSS mental distress with CDC's codebook, the hypertension cascade with NCHS Data Brief 511, and ACS totals with the Census Bureau's PUMS verification estimates. The data pipeline also reproduces a set of published figures on its cleaned store (Figure C.1). All are external benchmarks, not truth: a published figure carries its own sampling and processing error.
4. **Checks of mathematical claims.** Each interactive's claims and each derivation in [Appendix B](/appendix-b-derivations) are checked on seeded synthetic data.
5. **Determinism.** Rebuilding a chapter from the same inputs produces byte-identical facts, ledgers, and figures; only the manifest's timestamp changes.

## How the author rebuilds the booklet

These steps run on the author's machine against the store; the four promises at the top of this appendix say which of them a reader can repeat today.

1. **Data.** IPUMS extracts are defined in the pipeline's `definitions/` and downloaded and cleaned into the Box store's `analysis/` layer; NHANES is downloaded from NCHS and assembled by the NHANES scripts; BRFSS is downloaded from CDC as SAS transport files and harmonized by the BRFSS scripts. Microdata stay in the author's store.
2. **Artifacts.** In each chapter folder, `python build.py` reads only the columns it needs, computes every number, and writes `artifacts/`: `facts.json`, `ledger.json`, `figures/*.json`, and `manifest.json` with inputs and validation results. Optional `verify.R` scripts run the R checks.
3. **Export.** The integrator's `export.py` copies the artifacts and the chapter's MDX into the site repository, where each page reads its numbers from those files and never from typed text.
4. **Check.** `node scripts/check-mdx.mjs` compiles each page with the site's own MDX pipeline and confirms that every fact, figure, ledger, and citation it references exists.
5. **Deploy.** The static site is built with Next.js and deployed to Vercel under the `/weights` path, marked as a draft and excluded from search indexing.

## Terms of use

IPUMS data are used under the IPUMS terms of use: they are cited as each collection requests, and IPUMS microdata are never redistributed. The site publishes aggregates only: estimates, standard errors, intervals, counts, and the design facts in the field guide, each exported as a small JSON artifact. The same rule applies to NHANES and BRFSS, which are cited as their producers request. Microdata never leave the author's store, and nothing on the site allows a record to be reconstructed.

## Development history: the auditions

This section records how the empirical spine changed during drafting; reproducing a result does not require it. The proposal required each application to pass an audition before a chapter was written: the substantive question had to be worth answering on its own, and a central technical correction had to have a visible payoff. The auditions changed the spine.

One audition failed in the sense the proposal defined, and the failure is informative. In the ACS 2024 audition sample of full-time wage and salary workers, weighting barely moved the raw or the adjusted work-at-home wage contrast. The proposal's rule would have cut the application. The implementation plan recommends amending the rule so that an application also passes when a correction demonstrably does not change the answer and a reader would have assumed it would. That is Solon, Haider, and Wooldridge's own argument about when weighting matters <Cite id="solon2015" />, so [Chapter 4](/ch04-regression) reports the agreement and hands the interesting question, whose contrast the regression summarizes, to [Chapter 5](/ch05-hidden-weights). The two historical examples, the 1968 PSID poverty rate and the Wolfers divorce-law regressions, remain boxes: provenance, not headline applications, and neither is yet reproduced.
